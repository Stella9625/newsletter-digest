In this week’s newsletter abouthow early inventions could have appeared, I flagged the safety pin as an invention that could have been invented much earlier, and would have been useful had someone thought of it. The major inventions list (which I sourced from Wikipedia) lists the invention date for the safety pin as 1849, but Claude thought there was no reason that the safety pin couldn’t have been invented as early as 500 BC. Turns out Claude was right, andsafety pin-like devices were around 3000 years ago.

在本周关于早期发明本可如何出现的新闻通讯中，我将安全别针（safety pin）列为一项本可以更早发明、只要有人想到就会很有用的发明。那份重大发明清单（我从Wikipedia上获取的）将安全别针的发明日期列为1849年，但Claude认为，安全别针早在公元前500年就发明出来也并非不可能。事实证明Claude是对的，类似安全别针的装置在3000年前就已经存在。

This raises my confidence in the rest of Claude’s timeline estimates.

The process included rethinking the assembly line, which Ford helped to pioneer. That process is traditionally iterative, slow and depends on scores of outside partners. On Ford’s new “assembly tree,” a modular system stamps out two massive, aluminum castings and a battery that get merged at the end of the process—closer to how Tesla and China’s automakers build EVs. “We’ve never blown the whole thing up before and just started over,” Coffey said. “If and when we build this, we will rewire Ford.”” [WSJ] And another similar article in the New York Times. [NYT] Presumably some PR firm is earning their keep getting these articles placed.

There’s lots of debate about how fast the economy might grow if we truly develop Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) that can do anything a person can do. The big divide in this debate tends to be between the economists, who think growth rates might be a few percentage points, and the technologists, who think growth rates might be vastly higher. Here’s an interesting contribution to the debate, an estimate of how fast the economy could theoretically grow based on analysis of actual industrial production rates. “In this post, I compute the maximum rate at which an autonomous AI economy could grow, once its production is concentrated in the sectors most important for self-replication. I take the conservative case for this calculation: full automation, but no other technological improvement. Using US input-output data, I find this economy could double in about a year…” One interesting item in this analysis: under this rate of growth, construction would be a much larger share of GDP than it is currently. [Defenses in Depth]

关于如果我们真正开发出能做人类一切事情的通用人工智能（Artificial General Intelligence, AGI），经济增速可能有多快，目前存在大量争论。这场辩论的主要分歧通常在于经济学家和技术专家之间：前者认为增长率可能只有几个百分点，后者则认为增长率可能远高于此。这里有一项对该辩论的有趣贡献——基于对实际工业生产率的分析，估算经济理论上能以多快的速度增长。“在这篇文章中，我计算了一个自主AI经济一旦将生产集中于对自我复制最重要的部门时，所能达到的最高增速。我在计算中采取了保守假设：完全自动化，但没有其他技术进步。利用美国投入产出数据，我发现这个经济可以在大约一年内翻倍……”该分析中有一个有趣的发现：在这种增速下，建筑业占GDP（国内生产总值）的比重将远大于目前。[Defenses in Depth]

Luke Curley：WebRTC的AI语音困境
😤 批评
Quoting Luke Curley
WebRTC语音AI网络延迟丢包问题
Luke Curley指出，WebRTC为保证低延迟而激进丢弃音频包的设计虽适用于实时会议通话，却与AI语音场景存在根本矛盾——用户宁愿多等待片刻以确保大模型提示词准确完整，但浏览器端的WebRTC实现被硬编码追求实时性，根本不支持通过重传来换取数据完整性。
If you’ve ever heard distorted audio on a conference call, that’s WebRTC baybee.
要是你曾在电话会议里听到那种破音，那就是WebRTC干的好事，老弟。
After all, I’m paying good money to boil the ocean, and a garbage prompt means a garbage response.
毕竟我花大价钱是要干大事的，垃圾提示词就意味着垃圾回答。

Quoting Luke Curley

引用 Luke Curley

WebRTC is designed todegrade and drop my promptduring poor network conditions.

WebRTC（网页实时通信）的设计就是在网络状况不佳时主动降低质量并丢弃我的 prompt（提示词）。

wtf my dude

搞什么啊，老兄

WebRTC aggressively drops audio packets to keep latency low. If you’ve ever heard distorted audio on a conference call, that’s WebRTC baybee. The idea is that conference calls depend on rapid back-and-forth, so pausing to wait for audio is unacceptable.

WebRTC 会主动丢弃 audio packets（音频包）以保持低 latency（延迟）。如果你曾在电话会议中听到过失真音频，那就是 WebRTC 的“杰作”，老兄。其理念在于，电话会议依赖于快速的来回交互，因此暂停等待音频是不可接受的。

…but as a user, I would much rather wait an extra 200ms for my slow/expensive prompt to be accurate. After all, I’m paying good money to boil the ocean, and a garbage prompt means a garbage response. It’s not like LLMs are particularly responsive anyway.

……但作为一个用户，我宁愿多等 200 毫秒，让我这个又慢又贵的提示词变准确。毕竟，我可是花大价钱来“煮海”（boil the ocean）的，垃圾提示词意味着垃圾回复。而且 LLMs（大语言模型）本来也没快到哪去。

But I’m not allowed to wait. It’simpossibleto even retransmit a WebRTC audio packet within a browser; we tried at Discord. Theimplementationis hard-coded for real-time latencyor else.

但我不被允许等待。在浏览器内甚至无法重传 WebRTC 音频包；我们在 Discord 时就试过了。该 implementation（具体实现）被硬编码为追求 real-time（实时）低延迟，否则就玩完。

—Luke Curley, OpenAI’s WebRTC Problem, in response toHow OpenAI delivers low-latency voice AI at scale

——Luke Curley，《OpenAI 的 WebRTC 难题》，回应《OpenAI 如何大规模交付低延迟语音 AI》
